Step 2 · The rules

The charter

Five clauses, one document. If a clause does not read plainly, it is not finished. The rule you read here is the rule that runs.

EUR/USD H4 entry frame Long direction Valid
01

Market

One pair, one session

Trade EUR/USD on the H4 chart. Only bars that close within the window are candidates; a bar becomes visible only after it closes.

02

Entry

First retreat into a rising trend

Enter long when RSI(14) closes back above 40, after it has closed above 70 at least once in the prior 12 bars, with price holding above the 50-bar EMA.

03

Exit

Targets before stops

Close at a target 2.0× the entry risk, or at a stop one ATR(14) below entry, whichever the bar’s range genuinely orders first. When it cannot be ordered, the result is reported as a range — never guessed.

04

Risk filter

One position at a time

No new entry while an earlier position is open. Size each test trade at the risk budget set in Reality — sizing is decided there, not here.

No look-ahead

A bar becomes visible only after it closes. The engine has no way to read a partial bar — by design, not by convention.

Ambiguity is reported

When a bar’s range contains both stop and target and the data cannot order them, the trade is evaluated both ways and the study reports a range.

Picking the favourable branch is the single most common way backtesters lie. Assay refuses to.

Where fills are real

Spread, commission and slippage enter here as assumptions you set yourself in Reality — then every exit is measured against them.

Step 3 · The friction

Trading reality

A backtest is only as honest as the friction it assumes. Choose the shade of reality you want the numbers to live under — the engine then charges every trade accordingly.

These numbers will not shrink the drawdown. Whatever you choose here is what the engine charges. There is no “best case” toggle — a backtest that cannot survive its own costs is worthless, and Assay will not hide that.
