# Phase 0 validation kit: everyday traders

Use [the clickable prototype](everyday-traders-phase-0.html) in five moderated
20–30 minute sessions with rule-based traders who do not write trading code.
The simulation figures are intentionally illustrative research stimuli; do not
describe them as product results.

## Golden journey

1. Choose **Use a popular strategy** and use the EUR/USD 20/50 crossover.
2. Ask the participant to explain the long rule, short rule, entry timing, and
   risk setting in their own words.
3. Select **Conservative costs**. Ask what changed and why it matters.
4. Continue through the held-out period screen.
5. On the result, ask what the drawdown and 11-loss streak mean, whether the
   result predicts the next trade, and what the recommended next action is.

Also ask the participant to take the **Describe my own strategy** route, then
note the phrases that they expect the product to understand without help.

## Fixed verdict rubric

| Verdict | Evidence rule | Primary next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not testable | Rules are ambiguous or data is insufficient. | Clarify rules or obtain data. |
| Too little evidence | Too few independent trades or too little history. | Collect more history; do not optimize. |
| Unprofitable | Unseen-period expectancy is negative under conservative fills. | Discard or rewrite the hypothesis. |
| Fragile | Outcome depends heavily on costs, dates, or one exact setting. | Retest costs and nearby settings. |
| Promising | Positive unseen-period evidence with acceptable stability. | Paper trade a fixed version. |
| Strong evidence | Positive across periods, nearby settings, and realistic costs. | Paper trade; never call it proven. |

Every verdict must state: **Historical simulation only. This is not a forecast
or investment recommendation.**
